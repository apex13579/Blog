## Out of bandwidth

Between eighty-hour workweeks and grinding through networking certifications, I had to have an honest conversation with myself. The lab was demanding more time than I had left in the day. Rushed configurations at one in the morning, undocumented routing changes, and services held together by the promise of "I will fix it this weekend." 

Something had to give. The lab lost.

I am not writing a "see you in two weeks" post. I genuinely have no idea when I am coming back to active development, and I won't pretend otherwise. What I do know is that doing this halfway is worse than not doing it at all. It is technical debt with blinking lights. 

---

## Walking away from the keyboard

To be clear: I am not pulling the plugs. The rack isn't going dark. 

The servers will keep humming, and whatever is currently stable will just sit there and run. But the administrative work? The late-night troubleshooting, the new deployments, the endless tweaking? That is done. The freeze is administrative, not physical. I am simply taking my hands off the keyboard.

> **Win:** Knowing when to stop touching a production environment is a skill. Letting stable services run while you focus on other priorities is infinitely better than breaking things at 2 AM because you were too exhausted to read the documentation.

---

## The eventual rebuild

This is not quitting. 

When the time comes—and it will—I am burning it all down and starting over. Clean hardware, properly planned configurations, and actual documentation from day one. Everything I am learning right now in my certification tracks will get implemented the right way, rather than bolted onto a fragile setup after the fact. 

The rebuild will be better precisely because I won't be building it while running on fumes. The lab was always a means to an end, and that end is the knowledge and the career. Those are still moving.

---

## What keeps going

The blog stays online. I will keep writing when I have something worth saying.

Certification progress gets logged here. I might even use this extra time to tweak the site layout, add some visuals, and make this a place that feels less like a raw brain-dump and more like something you actually want to read.

What goes away for now is the hands-on hardware documentation. No deployment write-ups, no Proxmox troubleshooting sagas, no midnight config logs. 

---

## Retrospective

*   **Bank the money, finish the certs.** The hardware will wait. The knowledge does not stop accumulating just because I am not typing in the CLI. 
*   **A lab is a tool.** Never let the maintenance of the tool distract you from the reason you picked it up in the first place.
*   **Technical debt scales.** If you don't have the time to document it when you build it, you definitely won't have the time to troubleshoot it when it breaks.

I will be back. I don't know exactly when, but I promise the first post of the rebuild will be worth reading.

— Kyle
